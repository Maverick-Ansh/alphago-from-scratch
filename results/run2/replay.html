What the search was thinking

Why four claims fell

A 9×9 game ends in about a hundred moves. A rollout from move 80 therefore has twenty moves of noise left in it and essentially reads out the true result — measured MSE 0.02 by move 80, against a constant-predictor floor of 1.00. At 19×19 that same rollout has 250 moves to go and is genuinely noisy, which is the entire premise of the value network.

So C4 is that fact measured directly, C5 is it propagating into the search, and the collapse of the value-only player is it a third time. The value network is answering a question a small board does not ask. C1 and the pσ half of C7 are a separate story about the teacher: a policy distilled to 79% of a 128-simulation search's own self-agreement is still not a strong player.

What survives the resize is everything about learning from self-play — and the largest single effect measured anywhere here is policy-gradient RL taking one forward pass from worse than 50 rollouts to even with 1,000, at unchanged inference cost.

The bug worth the whole exercise

Two of three policy networks reported a test accuracy of 1.4%, frozen to four decimal places, with the test loss climbing past ln 81. The obvious explanation — too large a step size, rectifiers dying — fitted well enough to be written up and believed.

It was wrong. The three widths train at once against one shared feature cache, and open_memmap creates that cache at full size zero-filled before spending several seconds filling it. Whoever lost the race read zeros, copied a blank held-out set into memory at startup, and spent the entire run scoring itself against empty boards. The weights were fine the whole time — 22.65 / 22.85 / 23.20% when re-scored against a complete cache.

What broke it open was not a better theory. It was re-running the identical configuration and watching a different width survive. A mechanism that cannot predict which of three networks dies is not the mechanism. The step size it was blamed on turned out to be better one notch higher, not lower.
